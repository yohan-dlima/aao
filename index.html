Limits

  • An order shows the position on its own date. To find who administered an Act on a given date, set "Made on or before" under Narrow the search, read the newest order listed, then check whether a later amendment changed it.
  • Short amendments are not sorted by department. They say "omit" and "insert" instead of giving full lists. Find them with the full-text search.
  • Not every order is sorted by department. An order is only sorted when every check on it passes, because a wrong department is worse than none. Most orders before 1973, and some after, are word search only. The "All documents" tab shows the coverage of each one.
  • The older orders are scans. Letters are sometimes misread (for example "198l" for "1981"), so a search for an exact Act name can miss a document. Try a shorter or distinctive word.
  • Page numbers refer to the PDF, not the page number printed on the Gazette.
  • A long entry is sometimes split in two, or two entries joined, where the original ran over a line.
  • Misread letters have been put right where the same Act is printed correctly elsewhere (for example "Navigation Act [9!2" becomes "Navigation Act 1912"). Entries changed this way are marked "corrected": point at the mark to see what the scan read.
  • Some documents restate only some departments. The Act timeline carries the other departments on from the order before. A complete order that does not list an Act, when the complete orders either side of it do, is shown as a dashed bar: the line was most likely missed when the scan was read.

Known gaps

  • One early record held by the National Library of Australia (about 1901 to 1904) could not be downloaded.
  • Nothing after 1 February 2020 is included.
